CONCEPT MISSION // MARS

LIFE BEYOND EARTH.

ARES is a conceptual closed-loop habitat that combines aquaponics, water recovery, resource processing and subsurface protection to explore sustainable human settlement on Mars.

Explore ARES → How it works
01 // HABITAT

Live beneath the surface.

Mars is hostile to human life. ARES therefore places the primary habitat inside a protected subsurface environment, such as a lava tube or engineered underground structure.

ARES HABITAT
CONCEPTUAL ARCHITECTURE // NOT TO SCALE
☢️

Radiation Protection

Surrounding rock and regolith can provide passive shielding against the higher radiation environment experienced on the Martian surface.

🌡️

Thermal Stability

A subsurface location can reduce exposure to large surface temperature changes and lower the thermal burden on the habitat.

💧

Resource Processing

Extracted water can potentially support drinking, agriculture, oxygen production and other life-support functions after appropriate purification.

02 // CLOSED LOOP

Nothing useful gets wasted.

The aquaponics subsystem is designed around continuous resource cycling. Organic waste becomes plant nutrients, plants help clean the water, and treated water returns to the system.

STEP 01

Fish

Fish produce organic waste containing nutrients.

STEP 02

Biofilter

Beneficial bacteria transform ammonia into plant-available nitrogen compounds.

STEP 03

Plants

Plants absorb nutrients while helping remove compounds from the circulating water.

STEP 04

Filtration

Water passes through filtration and monitoring before being returned to the loop.

STEP 05

Habitat

Recovered resources support food production and human life-support operations.

04 // CHALLENGES

Mars doesn't make it easy.

A realistic Mars concept must acknowledge its limitations. ARES treats these problems as engineering challenges requiring further research and testing.

☢️

Radiation

Mars provides much less atmospheric and magnetic shielding than Earth.

→ Subsurface shielding + radiation monitoring

🧊

Extreme Cold

Low temperatures create significant thermal-control requirements for water and biological systems.

→ Insulation + active thermal management

🌬️

Low Pressure

The Martian atmosphere is too thin for humans and conventional agriculture to operate without pressure control.

→ Sealed, pressurized habitat

🧪

Perchlorates

Martian regolith contains perchlorate compounds that must be carefully managed before resources enter biological systems.

→ Multi-stage purification + monitoring

⚡

Power

Pumps, lighting, heating, filtration and life support require reliable continuous energy.

→ Reliable primary power + redundancy

🐟

Ecosystem Stability

Fish, bacteria and plants form an interconnected biological system where one failure can affect others.

→ Sensors + redundancy + isolation

♻️ Resource Efficiency

Recycle water and nutrients wherever practical.

🛡️ Redundancy

Critical life-support functions should not depend on a single point of failure.

🔬 Evidence First

Clearly distinguish measured data from simulations, estimates and future engineering targets.

05 // MISSION

Designing for the
long journey.

ARES explores one possible path toward sustainable human habitation beyond Earth: protect the crew, recycle resources, grow food and design every system with failure and recovery in mind.
